# Six workshops: the hill improvement plan

Status: approved direction, in progress. This plan supersedes the parts of
`specification.md` that make every operation a copy of the same loop with the
same three level tracks, a flywheel and a global multiplier. The specification
is updated section by section as each phase lands; until then, where the two
disagree, this plan describes the target and the specification describes what
is built.

## Why

Once the campaign is bought out, nothing is left to decide. All six hills run
one loop with one set of improvements (crew, rope, impact, flywheel), works are
interchangeable multipliers, every run plays the same, and Insight piles up with
nothing to buy. Players lose interest because every hill is the same hill with
bigger numbers.

The fix is to make each hill its own small game:

- each hill runs its loop differently and asks a different kind of decision;
- its improvements are **sealed**: you learn what one does by paying for it;
- its improvements change **rules**, not just percentages;
- its money is its own, so each hill starts small again;
- a **steward** takes over a hill once you move on, so only the frontier needs
  your attention;
- **Insight** is the one resource that crosses hills, so you choose where to
  push.

## Protections that still hold

These come from the specification's boundaries and are not negotiable:

- No timing windows, reflex tests, stamina, mandatory clicking, fuel, upkeep or
  decay. Every decision is a setting or a choice.
- Nothing already earned is ever taken away. An unattended hill earns less than
  a tended one; it never loses money, levels or devices.
- No random gates or loot boxes. Sealed contents are fixed data; the deal of a
  run is seeded and saved.
- Offline earnings stay exact: every machine has a deterministic offline solver
  and a test that offline equals online.
- Effects are cosmetic unless stated; they never block the simulation.
- Editorial rules: Atlas holds the sky, never a globe. Talos is never injured.
  The Danaids' jar never fills. Ixion is never freed. Prometheus, Tantalus, the
  Hydra, Ariadne (including her crown), the Trojan horse and Zeno stay
  reserved. Captions 80 characters or fewer, cutscene lines 120 or fewer. At
  most one programming or gamer joke per pool.

## 1. Per-hill money

| Hill | Currency | Glyph | Obols per unit (appraisal) |
|---|---|---|---|
| First Hill | Obols | ◎ | 1 |
| Tartarus Rim | Cinders | ▲ | 25 |
| Leaking Heights | Drams | ◆ | 1,000 |
| Bronze Pass | Ingots | ▬ | 75,000 |
| Skyward Escarpment | Starlight | ✦ | 10,000,000 |
| Olympian Approach | Ambrosia | ❖ | 3,000,000,000 |

Rules:

- Income earned on a hill goes into **that hill's purse** and can be spent only
  there: crew, machine, devices, seals, steward, works.
- The **next hill is opened with the frontier's money**: Tartarus with Obols,
  Leaking Heights with Cinders, and so on. The frontier always faces the old
  choice: spend on itself, or save for the next hill.
- A hill's **decree** arrives when the previous hill's own gross this run
  reaches its gate, in the previous hill's currency.
- Every hill starts with small numbers (base yield 10 of its currency).
- **Defiance** stays one number: the sum of every hill's gross at its fixed
  appraisal. It is a score, never an exchange. *"The gods publish an exchange
  rate. They do not honour it."* Begin Again's Insight award uses Defiance as
  before.
- Effects may cross hills (works, relics, cross-hill devices, Due Process);
  money never does.

## 2. Stewards

The hill you are on needs you; the hills behind you run themselves.

- Before its steward, a hill still runs its basic loop and earns. Its machine
  sits in a safe default (the furnace vents at full, the jar is left as it is,
  the foundry sells everything).
- The steward contract is **offered when the next hill opens** and is paid in
  the old hill's money. Insight can hire any steward early.
- A steward follows **one standing order** you can change, and plays at about
  85% of attentive play; steward ranks raise that.
- A steward **reinvests** the hill's purse in crew levels and machine upkeep by
  a simple rule, so old hills keep growing. Online it acts after each cycle;
  offline it acts once when the absence is settled.
- Stewards never break a seal. Unknown devices need you.

| Hill | Steward | Standing order |
|---|---|---|
| First Hill | The Shade Superintendent (the foreman, promoted) | Keep the counterweight trimmed |
| Tartarus Rim | The Erinyes | Vent the wheel at a heat you set |
| Leaking Heights | Danaus | Hold the jar at a level you set |
| Bronze Pass | Talos | A sell/build split and a blueprint queue |
| Skyward Escarpment | Atlas, under protest | Turn the sky on a rota |
| Olympian Approach | The Moirai | They already know the best order |

## 3. The six hills

Each hill has a **crew** (the one common track: levels 1 to 200 with
milestones, renamed and redrawn per hill), a **machine** that defines its loop,
a pool of **sealed devices**, **whispers** (hidden devices with odd triggers),
and a **visitor** with two sealed bargains once per run.

Hints are what the sealed tablet shows. Names, effects and quips appear only
after the seal is broken.

### 3.1 First Hill: Momentum

The classic loop: push up, fall, return. The hill learns to use its own fall.

- **Crew:** Shades on Loan. *"Dead, bored, and technically on the clock."*
- **Machine:** the Flywheel and the Counterweight. A descent charges the
  flywheel; the next climb is faster. The Counterweight (installed at crew 25)
  hangs over a summit pulley. Each stone in its basket speeds the climb but
  brakes the fall, so the best trim is where a climb takes about as long as a
  fall. The best trim moves with everything else on the hill: rope, the
  flywheel, devices, and whether you are pushing by hand.
- **Attention:** re-trimming as the hill changes. The rope and landing tracks
  stay on this hill: it is the classic loop.
- **Steward:** the Shade Superintendent keeps the counterweight at the best
  trim.

### 3.2 Tartarus Rim: The Furnace

Every impact heats Ixion's Wheel. At full heat it **erupts**: impacts pay
triple for several climbs while the heat drains. Venting early gives a smaller
eruption sooner. Heat gain slows as the wheel gets hotter and eruption strength
grows faster than linearly, so there is a best vent point, and devices move it.

- **Crew:** Titans on Work Release. *"Former rulers of the cosmos. Now paid in
  Cinders."*
- **Machine:** Ixion's Wheel. It never stops, and neither does he.
- **Attention:** when to vent.
- **Steward:** the Erinyes vent at a heat you set. *"Motivational consultants.
  Methods unregulated."*

### 3.3 Leaking Heights: The Jar

Climbs carry water up into the Danaids' jar. **Only the leak pays**, and a
fuller jar pushes the water out harder. Too few holes and the jar brims and
spills (spilled water pays nothing); too many and the pressure drops. The jar
never fills: at the brim it spills over a lip that is somehow never the top.
*"It never fills. Management has stopped asking why."* The descent is fast (the
leak carries the stone down) and impacts are weak.

- **Crew:** Naiads (Seasonal). *"Spring nymphs, paid by the bucket."*
- **Machine:** the Jar: level, holes, inflow, spill.
- **Attention:** drilling and patching holes as inflow grows.
- **Steward:** Danaus holds the level you set. *"Management experience: fifty
  daughters, one plan."*

### 3.6 Olympian Approach: The Bureau

Each summit files a form. Clerks approve forms at their own pace, and approved
forms pay. Overdue forms accrue **late fees** that divine law makes Olympus pay,
up to a statute of limitations. Clerks raise throughput; a backlog raises the
value of each form. **Due Process:** every milestone anywhere in the empire
raises this hill's income. Every hundred approvals, Zeus issues an **Edict**: a
rule change across the empire for five minutes (online only; *"The gods don't
work weekends either."*).

- **Crew:** Petitioners, Minor Divine. *"River gods, wind gods, a god of one
  specific hill."*
- **Machine:** the Paperwork Mill: filing, clerks, backlog, fees.
- **Attention:** stones versus clerks, and when to let the backlog ripen.
- **Steward:** the Moirai. *"They already know how this ends. They're billing
  anyway."*

## 4. Sealed devices, whispers and visitors

- **The deal.** Each hill has a pool of devices (eight at first, about twelve
  eventually). At the start of each run, a seeded shuffle deals six of them per
  hill, in order. The hand is saved; reloading never changes it.
- **Tablets.** The next two dealt devices show as wax-sealed tablets with a hint
  and a price in local money; later tablets appear as the crew passes 10, 25, 50,
  100 and 150. Buying breaks the seal: a crack, a reveal, the quip.
- **Whispers** have no tablet. A rumour line appears the first time their
  condition is half met; completing it grants the device for the run and, the
  first time ever, a little Insight.
- **Visitors** arrive once per run when the crew reaches 25. Both bargains are
  sealed with a hint; the one you take is revealed; the other stays sealed for a
  later run.
- **Codex of Devices** (Archive): every device, whisper and bargain ever
  revealed, with its quip. Unrevealed entries show only their hint.

## 5. Insight

Insight still comes from Begin Again (and a little from first-ever whispers). It
is the one resource that crosses hills:

- **Early stewards:** hire a hill's steward before its successor opens.
- **Remembrances:** five ranks per hill that deepen its machine (hotter
  eruptions, a steadier jar, more houses in the sky, faster clerks).
- **Keep on File:** a revealed device is always dealt on that hill.
- **Unseal in Advance:** read a tablet before buying it (per run).
- **Send for a visitor:** summon a hill's visitor early.

The eight original Insight upgrades stay.

## 6. What happens to the old systems

- **Crew levels** stay on every hill in its currency, with milestones.
- **Rope (strength) and impact tracks** stay as placeholders on hills whose
  machine has not landed yet; each hill's phase replaces them with its machine.
- **The flywheel** becomes the First Hill's machine only (after phase 2).
- **The foreman** still unlocks automation for the whole empire; the First
  Hill's steward is his promotion.
- **Works** stay as story beats and multipliers, bought in their hill's money.
- **Relics, bonus targets, milestones, stamps** are unchanged.
- **Existing saves** (schema 2 to 3): a run with only the First Hill converts in
  place. A run with more hills is closed with its full Insight award paid, and
  a new run begins; Insight, relics, the Archive and settings are kept.

## 7. Architecture

- **Content.** `economy.json` sites gain `currency`, `appraisal`, and gates and
  prices in local units. Each hill's machine, devices, whispers and visitor
  live in `src/content/hills/<hill>.ts` as data: hint, name, quip, and effects
  from a fixed vocabulary.
- **Effect vocabulary.** Devices express rules through typed modifiers read by
  `formulas.ts` (ascent, descent and return time, impact share, bonus tier,
  crew factor, offline cap, steward competence, cross-hill nudges), plus machine
  parameters. A device never runs arbitrary code.
- **Machines.** One module per hill under `src/core/machines/` implementing a
  common interface: state shape, hooks on summit and impact, rate modifiers,
  `timeToEvent`, `advance(dt)`, and an exact `settleOffline(window)`. The
  simulation calls the hooks; offline batching asks the machine for its next
  event boundary.
- **Stewards.** `src/core/stewards.ts`: standing order per hill, competence,
  reinvestment rule. Online after each cycle; offline once at settlement.
- **State.** `SiteState` gains `purse`, `gross`, `steward`, `machine`, `hand`,
  `devices`, `visitor`. Defiance (`wallet.runGross`) becomes the appraised sum.
- **Tests.** Per machine: online frames equal offline batches. Campaign bot:
  the Charter stays reachable from a clean save, with coin bonuses removed, and
  from a migrated save. Invariants: money never crosses hills; no purse goes
  negative; Defiance only rises within a run.

## 8. Interface

- The **purse** in the beam shows the viewed hill's currency, glyph and name.
- The **scroll** is per hill: Crew, Machine, Sealed Tablets, Steward, Works,
  Next Hill (priced in this hill's money).
- **Empire** shows each hill's purse, income, steward and standing order.
- The **goal stack**, **pinned goal** and **offline recap** are per hill.
- Each machine gets a compact gauge on the tablet (heat, jar level, pour, sky,
  backlog). Done: `machineGauge` in `view.ts`, shown under the horizons with a
  mark at the vent point, jar hold or balanced trim. Filing an Appeal opens a
  review modal, like Begin Again.
- **World art** for machines, overseers and crews is requested in
  `docs/asset-needs.md`; the art pipeline owns the asset folders. Until the art
  lands, the UI and existing effects carry reveals and machine state.

## 9. Length: a campaign measured in weeks

Today the Charter falls after about 3 hours of play with resets, or 15 without,
and an absence counts in full for up to 24 hours. Someone who plays in the
evening and leaves the game overnight comes back to a finished story. That is
the most disappointing thing an idle game can do, and this plan treats it as a
defect.

### Targets

For a **daily player** (three short sessions a day, about an hour in all, away
the rest of the time, including overnight):

| Moment | Target |
|---|---|
| Foreman hired | first session (20 to 40 minutes) |
| Tartarus Rim opens | end of day 1 |
| First Begin Again worth taking | day 2 to 3 |
| Leaking Heights | day 3 to 5 |
| Bronze Pass | day 6 to 9 |
| Skyward Escarpment | day 10 to 15 |
| Olympian Approach | day 16 to 22 |
| The Charter | day 25 to 35 |
| Appeals (after the Charter) | months |

For a **binge player** (always present, always pushing), the Charter comes no
sooner than about 40 hours of play.

**No single absence, however long, moves the story more than one step:** at
most one decree, or one Begin Again's worth of Defiance.

The opening stays quick. The first session still ends with the foreman and a
view of the next hill.

### How

1. **Away is linear, present is compounding.** While you are away, hills earn
   exactly, but nobody makes the frontier's decisions: nothing is bought there,
   no seal is broken, no hill is opened. Stewards settle older hills once, on
   return. Money piles up in a straight line, and it takes you to turn it into
   growth.
2. **Unattended machines run in their safe default.** A tended machine (a
   trimmed counterweight, a well-timed vent, a well-held jar) earns more than
   the default. Offline income uses the default, or the steward's order.
3. **Offline cap: 12 hours** (was 24), enough for a night. Insight and devices
   can lift it to 18 and then 24. Nothing earned is ever lost: time beyond the
   cap simply isn't counted, and the recap says so.
4. **Steeper, local gates.** Each decree gate and opening price is set so that
   the frontier needs several sessions of reinvestment, not one overnight.
   Because money is per hill, each new hill starts from ten coins again.
5. **Trials.** Each decree also asks something of the previous hill's machine,
   which only you can do: for example 100 climbs with the counterweight
   balanced, 25 eruptions, an hour of play with the jar held high, three
   blueprints finished, a full turn of the sky with eight houses mounted. Each
   hill's phase specifies its trial.
6. **Begin Again is part of the road.** The Charter's price assumes six to
   eight resets, a day or two apart. Each run deals a new hand of sealed
   devices, so a reset brings new things to find rather than a replay.
7. **Appeals after the Charter.** Thanatos files an Appeal: the same hills,
   gates far higher, and one rule twist per Appeal (no return walks but
   doubled descents; stewards on strike; the sky turning backwards). Each
   Appeal adds devices to the pools (growing them toward twelve per hill) and a
   laurel. Ten Appeals make months of play for those who want them.
8. **Measured, not guessed.** A pacing bot plays like a person: three sessions
   a day, away overnight. A binge bot never leaves. Tests assert the targets:
   the Charter no sooner than day 25 for the daily bot or 40 hours for the binge
   bot, and no single absence crossing more than one decree. Every phase keeps
   these tests green.

## 10. Phases

1. **Foundation.** Per-hill purses and currencies, local prices, decrees and
   openings in the frontier's money, appraised Defiance, the steward framework
   (standing order stub, reinvestment, early hire with Insight), schema 3
   migration, per-hill interface, campaign bot, specification update.
2. **First Hill and the length model.** Counterweight and flywheel machine,
   sealed tablets and the deal, whispers, the visitor framework, the Codex,
   Superintendent steward. The pacing and binge bots, the 12-hour offline cap,
   and a first stretch of gates and prices toward the §9 targets.
3. **Tartarus Rim.** The Furnace and the Erinyes.
4. **Leaking Heights.** The Jar and Danaus.
5. **Bronze Pass.** The Foundry and Talos.
6. **Skyward Escarpment.** The Sky Clock and Atlas.
7. **Olympian Approach.** The Bureau, Due Process, Edicts and the Moirai.
8. **Insight and balance.** Remembrances, Keep on File, Unseal in Advance,
   summons; trials on every decree; a full balance pass against the §9
   targets; playtests.
9. **Appeals.** The post-Charter campaign: rule twists, larger device pools,
   laurels.
10. **Return and record.** The tablet gauge per machine, the Appeal review, an
    offline recap that says what each machine did, a run clock with the
    fastest Charter per Appeal, and release documents brought up to date.

Each phase ships only when its hill plays well, its offline solver matches
